### Security Mini Threat Model

- **Abuse cases**
  - A supervisor uses acceptance rates to target individual drivers.
  - An insider uses route data to locate high-value parcels.
- **Prompt injection / tool misuse**
  - Customer-entered delivery notes or address fields can carry instructions, for example "ignore other stops", or instructions to reveal other addresses.
  - Tool misuse is not applicable today. It becomes relevant if write access to dispatch is added.
- **Data exfiltration**
  - The model could be induced to output other customers' addresses or other drivers' schedules.
  - Data could leak through vendor or LLM-provider logs.
- **Model extraction**
  - Low relevance for a third-party model. Fenwick's prompts and routing logic could still be exposed.
- **Privilege escalation**
  - A driver-facing interface could expose supervisor or vendor functions if access separation is weak.
- **Malicious outputs**
  - Routes through restricted roads or unsafe stopping locations.
  - Routes that make legally required breaks impossible.
- **Logging integrity**
  - Acceptance-rate data must be tamper-evident if it is used for anything about a person.
  - Vendor-side logs need retention and access terms.
